About the artist

Paul Sergounine

Paul Sergounine (b. 1984) is a contemporary painter based in Tbilisi, Georgia, with strong ties to Düsseldorf, Germany. He is represented by Galerie Bruno Massa.

Raised in Düsseldorf, Sergounine graduated from the International School of Düsseldorf in 2003. He subsequently studied at Webster University in Leiden and ISG in Paris before completing a BA in International Management at Webster University Vienna in 2008. Following years of international experience, he established his studio in Tbilisi in 2021.

His ongoing body of work, Mind Mechanics (2025–), is conceived as a cycle of 250 paintings across five predetermined formats. Investigating consciousness, memory, perception and identity, it employs recurring forms as structural elements through which psychological complexity is explored.

Initially developed through oil stick and oil pastel on black acrylic grounds, his practice has expanded towards increasingly physical and experimental approaches. Material excavation, cannibalisation of earlier works and assemblage have introduced new dimensions while preserving its conceptual foundation.

In parallel, Totems (2025–) explores related concerns through vertical, architectural compositions.

Statement

I do not paint to decorate, illustrate, or comfort.

I paint to expose the unstable architecture of thought.

My work investigates the structures through which consciousness, memory, perception and identity take shape.

Rather than depicting external reality, I explore how experiences accumulate, fragment, erode and reorganise over time.

In Mind Mechanics, recurring forms establish a visual framework in which order and instability coexist.

Alongside this psychological investigation, my practice has evolved towards a more physical and experimental approach.

I am interested not only in what a painting expresses, but also in what can be discovered through direct intervention.

The canvas becomes a place of exploration, where deliberate actions lead to consequences I cannot always anticipate.

Working with acrylic ground, oil stick and oil pastel, I build successive layers that are repeatedly altered, concealed and exposed.

Scraping allows me to uncover what lies beneath, bringing previously separated pigments into contact and revealing unexpected relationships between earlier gestures and new interventions.

In this sense, my works function as palimpsests, compositions in which traces of previous states survive beneath, and sometimes within, what has replaced them.

Like memory itself, the image retains evidence of its past even as its structure continues to change.

These discoveries influence the direction of the painting, allowing the process itself to challenge my original intentions.

Beginning with Mind Mechanics No. 56, I introduced the cannibalisation of former works, expanding my practice towards greater physical depth and dimensionality.

Paintings that no longer satisfy me are cut apart, their fragments becoming elements of new compositions.

By reusing parts of former works, I allow their histories to continue in another form rather than disappear entirely.

I also began incorporating spray paint, parchment paper, newspaper and pages from books, extending the material possibilities of the canvas.

This development is particularly evident in Mind Mechanics No. 58, where different materials are brought together through assemblage, without incorporating fragments of previous paintings.

I have no interest in refinement for its own sake, particularly when it erases the evidence of decisions made in the moment.

I leave marks hurried, corrected or imperfect, allowing the process to remain visible.

For me, spontaneity does not exclude rigour; it demands an openness to what cannot be fully controlled.

My paintings do not seek definitive answers.

They remain investigations of consciousness, shaped equally by intention, physical action and the discoveries that emerge between them.
